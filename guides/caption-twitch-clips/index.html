How to add word-by-word captions to Twitch clips for free

Lots of people scroll short-form video with the sound off, and a stream clip with no captions loses them in the first second. You don't need a paid caption app: two free tools, Whisper and FFmpeg, get you the bold word-by-word style you see on TikTok and Shorts.

1. Trim the clip first

Cut the moment out of the VOD before you transcribe anything. Transcribing a 30-second clip takes seconds; transcribing a six-hour stream takes a long time and most of it gets thrown away. If you still need to find the moment, start with the finding highlights guide.

2. Transcribe with word timestamps

Install open-source Whisper (or faster-whisper, a free reimplementation that runs faster) and turn on word-level timestamps. That gives you each word with its own start and end time, which is what makes word-by-word captions possible.

whisper clip.mp4 --model small --word_timestamps True --output_format json

The small model is a good balance of speed and accuracy on a normal laptop. Use a larger one if your mic is noisy or game audio is loud.

3. Fix names, game terms and slang

Speech recognition gets streamer names, game terms and chat slang wrong all the time. Fix those in the transcript before styling. A caption that misspells the streamer's own name is the fastest way to look low effort.

4. Show two to four words at a time

Full sentences on screen are hard to read on a phone. Group words into short chunks and highlight the word being spoken, either with a color change or a slight size bump. Break chunks at natural pauses so a line never ends in the middle of a phrase.

5. Style it as an .ass subtitle file

The .ass subtitle format supports fonts, outlines, colors and per-word timing, so it's the easiest way to get the TikTok look out of FFmpeg. A few rules that hold up on every app:

  • Bold, heavy font with a thick dark outline, so it reads over bright game footage.
  • Middle of the frame or just below it. The bottom of the screen gets covered by the app's caption, username and buttons.
  • Big enough to read at arm's length, roughly the width of a thumb per word on a 1080x1920 frame.
  • One highlight color for the active word, and keep it consistent across all your clips so people recognize your channel.

6. Burn the captions in with FFmpeg

Burn the captions into the video rather than uploading a separate subtitle file. That way they look the same on TikTok, Shorts and Reels.

ffmpeg -i clip_vertical.mp4 -vf "subtitles=captions.ass" -c:a copy clip_captioned.mp4

Do this after you crop to vertical 9:16, so the caption positions match the final frame. The webcam over gameplay guide covers the cropping, and the titles and hooks guide covers the on-screen text for the first two seconds.
