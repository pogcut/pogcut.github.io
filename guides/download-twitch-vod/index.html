How to download your Twitch VOD before it expires

Twitch doesn't keep your streams forever. If a great moment is sitting in last week's broadcast, you may have only days left to save it. Here's how to download your own VODs for free, or grab just the part you want to clip.

1. Make sure Twitch is saving your streams

Past broadcast storage is off by default. In the Creator Dashboard, go to your stream settings and turn on Store past broadcasts. Only streams made after you switch it on get saved, so do this before your next stream.

2. Know your deadline

Twitch's help center lists these retention windows for past broadcasts:

  • Partners, Turbo and Prime users: 60 days.
  • Affiliates: 14 days.
  • Everyone else: 7 days.

Two catches: if you stream in 2K or dual-format vertical, those extra versions expire after 7 days even when the regular 1080p VOD lasts longer. Highlights and uploads stay indefinitely but share a 100-hour storage cap, so turning every stream into a Highlight isn't a long-term backup.

3. Download from the Video Producer

The official way: in the Creator Dashboard open Content, then Video Producer. Find the past broadcast, open its three-dot menu and choose Download. Twitch prepares the file and it downloads at the quality you streamed. It's one video at a time, and a four-hour 1080p60 stream can be many gigabytes.

4. Or grab only the highlight section with yt-dlp

If you only need a few minutes, the free, open-source yt-dlp tool can download just that slice of a VOD. Copy the VOD link from your channel and give it the start and end times:

yt-dlp --download-sections "*1:02:30-1:05:00" -o "highlight.%(ext)s" "https://www.twitch.tv/videos/VOD_ID"

This saves 2 minutes 30 seconds starting at 1:02:30 instead of the full stream. Start a few seconds before the moment you remember; the setup is what makes a clip land. To list the available qualities first, run yt-dlp -F with the same link. Only download VODs you own or have the streamer's permission to use.

5. Keep the original quality

Save the source quality, usually 1080p60. You'll crop it down to a 9:16 frame for TikTok and Shorts, so starting from a soft 720p file makes the final clip blurry. Keep the original frame rate too; fast games look noticeably better at 60fps.
