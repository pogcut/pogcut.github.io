How to find the best moments in a Twitch VOD

A six-hour stream might have five moments worth posting. Rewatching the whole thing to find them takes longer than the stream did. Your chat already flagged the good parts for you; here's how to read the signal.

1. Get the chat replay

Every Twitch VOD keeps its chat replay. Export it with a free tool such as TwitchDownloader so you have each message with a timestamp. That file is your highlight map.

2. Look for chat spikes, not just busy chat

Count messages in short windows (10 to 30 seconds) across the whole stream. What matters is how much busier a window is compared to that stream's normal pace. A small channel going from 3 messages to 30 is as strong a signal as a big channel going from 300 to 3,000.

3. Weight the reaction emotes

Not all chat activity is a reaction. A raid or a giveaway can flood chat without anything funny happening. Give extra weight to messages that are clearly reactions:

  • Funny: KEKW, LUL, OMEGALUL, LMAO, ICANT
  • Hype: Pog, PogChamp, POGGERS, EZ Clap, "W"
  • Tension or fail: monkaS, "???", F, "L"

Include BTTV, 7TV and FFZ emotes. On many channels those carry most of the reactions, so counting only Twitch's built-in emotes misses a lot.

4. Confirm with audio and the transcript

A chat spike tells you where to look. The audio and transcript tell you whether the moment works on its own. Loud reactions, laughter, a quotable line, or a sudden silence are good signs. If the moment only makes sense with 20 minutes of context, skip it; short-form viewers won't have that context.

5. Start before the spike

Viewers watch on a delay and need a few seconds to type, so the burst of messages lands after the action. Start the clip several seconds earlier so the setup is in it, and end right after the payoff. Trailing reactions can usually go.

6. Rank, cut and post

Keep the strongest handful of moments rather than every spike. Crop each to vertical 9:16 with your face cam and the action both visible, add captions, and write a title and opening hook. The titles and hooks guide covers that part, and the VOD to TikTok guide walks through the editing.
