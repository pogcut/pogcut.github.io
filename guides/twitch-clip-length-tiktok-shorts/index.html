How long should Twitch clips be for TikTok, Shorts and Reels?

Platforms will take a long file. New viewers will not. Here's the practical length for stream highlights, what each app allows, and a free way to trim a vertical clip to that target.

1. The sweet spot: 15–30 seconds (up to ~60)

For cold traffic on TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, most Twitch moments hold best at about 15 to 30 seconds. That is long enough for a setup and a punchline, and short enough that swipe-away viewers still see the payoff. Stretch toward ~60 seconds when the joke needs a beat of context, a chat callout, or a clutch play that takes time to land. Longer than that is fine for fans who already follow you; it rarely wins discovery.

  • 15–30s — one clear moment: reaction, fail, win, or one-liner.
  • ~45–60s — short story with a setup; cut every second of dead air.
  • Over 60s — only when the whole arc is the point, and usually after you already have a tighter cut for Shorts/TikTok.

2. What each platform allows

Hard caps are ceilings, not goals. Retention still decides whether the algorithm keeps showing the clip.

PlatformHard limitPractical target for stream clips
TikTokUploads can run many minutes (see TikTok Help)15–30s discovery; up to ~60s when needed
YouTube ShortsUp to 3 minutes (YouTube Help)Under 60s, especially with game/stream music
Instagram ReelsUp to 20 minutes; Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to new audiences (Instagram Help)15–45s for growth
Twitch native clipUp to 60 seconds (Twitch Help)Use the full window only if every second earns it

One Shorts-specific trap: a Short over one minute with any active copyright claim is blocked globally. Game soundtracks and music on stream often trip Content ID. Details and workarounds are in our YouTube Shorts guide. For Reels reach rules and export size, see the Instagram Reels guide.

3. Twitch's 60-second ceiling

According to Twitch Help, the Clip Creator lets you pick a moment up to 60 seconds. That matches the sweet spot above, but it also means a 90-second story will not fit in one native clip. Options:

  • Cut from the VOD. Download that section, then trim and crop to vertical.
  • Join two clips. Make back-to-back 60s clips and concat them with FFmpeg (command in the how to clip on Twitch guide).
  • Make a tight Shorts/TikTok cut plus a longer cut for subscribers who want the full bit.

4. Front-load the hook, cut dead air, end on the reaction

Length only helps if the first second earns the next. For stream highlights:

  1. Start 1–2 seconds before the action — not five seconds of walking or silence.
  2. Put the question or payoff on screen immediately (on-screen text + captions). Templates live in the titles and hooks guide.
  3. Cut pauses, loading screens and "uhh" between beats. Dead air is the fastest swipe.
  4. End on the reaction — the laugh, the scream, the chat spam — then stop. Do not linger on the aftermath unless that is the joke.

5. Free FFmpeg trim to a target length

If you already have a vertical 1080x1920 60fps file and just need a shorter cut, this free FFmpeg command keeps the resolution and frame rate and takes 25 seconds starting at 0:03:

ffmpeg -ss 3 -i vertical.mp4 -t 25 -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset medium -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k -movflags +faststart clip-25s.mp4

Change -ss for the start time (seconds) and -t for the duration. If you still need to crop from a 16:9 stream at the same time, use the export command in the Shorts guide or the webcam-over-gameplay layout. Burn in word-by-word captions before you upload — many people watch muted.
