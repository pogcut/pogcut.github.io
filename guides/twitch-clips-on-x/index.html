How to post Twitch clips on X (Twitter)

A lot of streamers already live on X: go-live posts, replies, memes. Clips are the post type that actually shows people what your stream feels like. Here are the limits, an export command, and how to write the post so the clip gets watched.

1. Know the limits

  • Length: free X accounts can upload videos up to 140 seconds (2:20). Premium raises that a lot, but you don't need it for highlights.
  • File size: up to 512 MB on a free account. A one-minute 1080p60 clip is usually a few tens of MB, so size is rarely the problem.
  • Format: MP4 (or MOV) with H.264 video and AAC audio, up to 60fps. Landscape, square and vertical all work.

Two minutes is a ceiling, not a target. Start right before the setup and end right after the payoff; most stream moments land in 15–45 seconds. See how long Twitch clips should be for the reasoning.

2. Export one file that works everywhere

X takes vertical video, so the same 1080x1920 clip you make for TikTok and Shorts can go straight on X. This free FFmpeg command takes a 45-second slice starting at 0:05 from a 16:9 stream recording, center-crops it to vertical and exports an X-friendly MP4 at 60fps:

ffmpeg -ss 5 -i clip.mp4 -t 45 -vf "crop=ih*9/16:ih,scale=1080:1920,fps=60" -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -crf 20 -preset medium -g 120 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 128k -ar 44100 -movflags +faststart x-clip.mp4

-profile:v high and yuv420p keep the file in the H.264 flavor X expects, -g 120 puts a keyframe every two seconds, and +faststart lets playback begin before the whole file loads. We tested it on a 1080x1920 60fps PogCut clip: 20 seconds came out at about 10 MB. If your clip is already vertical, drop the crop=..., part. Want your webcam on top? Use the webcam-over-gameplay layout.

3. Upload the video, don't paste the link

A pasted Twitch clip link shows up as a link card, and people have to leave X to watch. A natively uploaded video autoplays in the timeline, which is where the views come from. Upload the MP4 and put your Twitch link in the text or in a reply under the post.

4. Write one line of setup

The post text does the job a title does on Shorts: set up the moment without spoiling it.

  • Tease the stakes: "chat told me to concede. I had one card left."
  • Ask the question the clip answers: "how did this not kill me"
  • Keep it short, lowercase is fine, and skip a wall of hashtags.

Burn in word-by-word captions too, since timeline videos start muted. More templates are in the titles and hooks guide.

5. Post while the stream is fresh

Post the best clip shortly after you go offline, while your community is still talking about it, and pin it if it's a good intro to your channel. Tag the game or anyone who was in the moment when it's natural. For timing across platforms, see how often to post clips.
