How to turn a Twitch VOD into TikTok, Shorts and Reels clips

Your best stream moments are buried in hours of footage. Here's how to find them, cut them into vertical clips, and give each one a title and hook that keeps people watching.

1. Find the highlights

The fastest signal is your chat. When something funny or hype happens, chat speeds up and fills with emotes like LUL, KEKW, OMEGALUL or POG. Scrub the VOD for those spikes, plus:

  • Big reactions: screams, laughing fits, rage, stunned silence.
  • Clutch plays, fails and close calls.
  • Lines that are funny without any backstory. If a stranger needs context to get it, it probably won't travel.

Write down the timestamp of each moment you find.

2. Get the footage

For a quick cut, Twitch's built-in clip tool works, though clips are short. For full control, download the VOD itself: in your Creator Dashboard, go to Content, then Video Producer, and download the past broadcast. Twitch deletes past broadcasts after a limited time, so save the streams you want before they expire.

3. Trim to the moment

Aim for about 15 to 60 seconds. The most common mistake is a slow start: viewers decide whether to keep watching in the first couple of seconds, so open as close to the payoff as you can. Cut the setup down to what's needed to understand it.

4. Crop to vertical 9:16

TikTok, Shorts and Reels are vertical (1080x1920). A stream is horizontal (16:9), so you need to reframe it. The layout most streamers use stacks the webcam on top and a zoomed-in slice of gameplay below. If there's no webcam, crop to the action or put the gameplay over a blurred background. Free editors like CapCut can do this by hand.

5. Add captions

Lots of people scroll with the sound off. Burn in big, high-contrast captions that follow what's being said, and keep them away from the very bottom of the frame, where each app puts its buttons and text.

6. Write a title and hook that hold viewers

A good short-form title creates a curiosity gap: it teases what happens without giving it away. Some patterns that work:

  • Consequence tease: "He trusted chat one time… one time"
  • Stakes: "1 HP and he still went for it"
  • Chat reaction: "Chat called it 10 seconds before it happened"

Keep it short, conversational and true to the clip. A title that oversells the moment gets people to click and then leave early.

7. Post and send viewers back to your stream

Upload to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, and mention your Twitch channel in the caption or on your profile so new viewers can find you live. Posting regularly matters more than any single clip.
