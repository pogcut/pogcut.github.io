Twitch clip titles and hooks that keep people watching

On TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Reels, the clip itself is only half the job. The first two seconds and the words on screen decide whether anyone sticks around to see your best moment. Here's how to write both.

1. The hook is the first two seconds

Short-form viewers decide almost instantly whether to keep watching. Your opening frame should already have something going on:

  • Start mid-action: the boss at low health, the enemy rounding the corner, the moment chat starts typing.
  • Start with a line that raises a question, like "Okay, I have a terrible idea."
  • Cut the warm-up. If the first few seconds are you explaining the setup, trim it or cover it with on-screen text.

2. Use on-screen text as a second hook

Put a short line of text near the top of the frame for the first few seconds. It does the job a thumbnail does on long videos: it tells the viewer why this clip is worth their time. Keep it to a handful of words and make sure it doesn't cover your face cam or the action.

3. Write titles with a curiosity gap

A curiosity gap tells people something is about to happen without telling them what. These patterns work well for stream highlights:

  • Stakes: "1 HP and he still went for it"
  • Bad decision: "He trusted chat one time… one time"
  • Chat called it: "Chat saw it coming 10 seconds before he did"
  • Understatement: "This went slightly wrong"
  • Challenge: "Wait for the last 3 seconds"
  • Rule broken: "Never do this in ranked"
  • Quote: use the funniest line from the clip, word for word, in quotes
  • Reaction: "I genuinely can't believe that worked"

4. Keep it honest

A title that oversells the moment gets the click and then loses the viewer, and platforms notice when people leave early. Tease the moment you actually have. If the clip is a slow-burn joke, say so with the title ("Wait for it") instead of promising an explosion.

5. Let the platform know what it is

The title's job is curiosity; the description and hashtags tell the platform who to show it to. Add the game, the type of moment (clutch, fail, rage, funny), and your channel name so new viewers can find you live on Twitch.

6. Test and repeat what works

Post several clips from the same stream with different hook styles, then compare how long people watched, not just views. Once a pattern works for your audience, reuse it. Consistency beats any single viral clip.
