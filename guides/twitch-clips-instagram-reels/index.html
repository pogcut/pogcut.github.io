How to post Twitch clips as Instagram Reels

Instagram Reels puts your stream highlights in front of people who'd never browse Twitch. Here's how long a Reel should be, the size to export, and a free command that turns a Twitch clip into an upload-ready file.

1. Stay under three minutes

Instagram Help says you can make Reels up to 20 minutes, but Reels over 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences. Since the point of posting highlights is reaching people who don't follow you yet, treat three minutes as a hard ceiling. Most stream moments do best when they start right at the setup and end just after the payoff, often in 15 to 45 seconds.

2. Use the right size

Per Instagram's Reel size guidance, Reels can be anywhere from 1.91:1 to 9:16, with at least 30fps and 720 pixels. For a full-screen Reel, export 9:16 at 1080x1920. Keeping your stream's 60fps is fine and makes fast gameplay look smoother.

3. Export the Reel with FFmpeg

This free FFmpeg command takes 30 seconds starting at 0:05, crops the center of a 1080p stream to vertical, scales it to 1080x1920 and keeps 60fps. We tested it on a 1920x1080 60fps source:

ffmpeg -ss 5 -i clip.mp4 -t 30 -vf "crop=ih*9/16:ih,scale=1080:1920,fps=60" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -preset medium -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k -movflags +faststart reel.mp4

A center crop suits full-screen gameplay or just-chatting. For your webcam on top and gameplay below, use the webcam-over-gameplay layout guide. Need the footage first? See how to download your Twitch VOD.

4. Captions and the first second

Lots of people scroll Reels muted, so burn in word-by-word captions. Put a short line of text on screen in the first second that sets up the payoff, and keep text in the middle of the frame: the bottom is covered by your caption, username and audio label, and the right edge by the like and share buttons. Our titles and hooks guide has hook templates that work for stream moments.

5. Upload a clean file

Upload the file you exported rather than a download from another app with its watermark on it. In the caption, name your Twitch channel so viewers can find you live. The same 1080x1920 file also works for YouTube Shorts and TikTok.
